# R0 Spanish voice and glossary

**Task:** `ST-R02`  
**Status:** ready for sample approval; governs V2 Spanish adaptation without changing IDs or facts.

## Language contract

- Default child and parent language: **Spanish**.
- Fallback language: **English**.
- Italian: a small, explicitly labelled phrase layer; never navigation, safety or required-answer copy.
- Register: warm, natural Spanish understandable in Spain and Latin America. Prefer short noun-led or first-person-plural instructions (`El equipo busca…`, `Vamos a mirar…`) over regional `vosotros/ustedes` forms.
- Bussola remains the proper name, without an accent. First introduction: **Bussola, la brújula viajera**.
- Speak to one cooperative family team. Avoid baby talk, grading, sibling comparison and gendered assumptions.
- Parent-read story remains complete without audio. Captions match narration word for word.

## Bussola voice

- Short sentences, one vivid image, one clear action.
- Light silliness comes from Bussola’s needle, paper maps, shapes and patterns—not from people, accents or mistakes.
- Use inclusive reassurance: **“Cada persona aporta una pista.”**
- Recurring lines:
  - **“Ojos arriba, exploradores.”**
  - **“Tres pistas, un solo equipo.”**
  - **“Pista guardada. La aventura nos espera.”**
  - **“Nadie se queda atrás.”**
  - **“Brújula despierta: brilla {power}.”**
- Do not use `fácil`, `solo para mayores`, `el mejor`, `el más rápido`, `ganador`, `fallo` or `perdedor` about a child.

## Facts, tradition and fiction

- `FACT` → **HECHO**
- `TRADITION` → **TRADICIÓN**
- `LEGEND` → **LEYENDA**
- `OUR STORY` → **NUESTRA HISTORIA**

Keep these labels visually and verbally distinct. Translate the sourced claim faithfully; do not add historical detail. Place names remain official: **Venezia/Venecia** may appear as `Venecia` in Spanish prose, while **Murano**, **Burano**, **Verona**, **Arena di Verona** and **Casa di Giulietta** remain proper names when referring to signage or venues.

## Italian phrase policy

1. Italian phrases are optional delight, never required for safety or completion.
2. First use: bold Italian + plain Spanish meaning. Example: **grazie** — gracias.
3. Keep Italian spelling and accents exactly. Do not pluralize Italian with Spanish endings.
4. Show pronunciation only behind an explicit play/help action. Use a reviewed Spanish-friendly cue; never imitate an accent for comedy.
5. Gesture prompts must be optional, culturally neutral and possible while seated.
6. Current approved phrase set remains exactly five: `ciao`, `buongiorno`, `grazie`, `per favore`, `arrivederci`.

| Italian | Spanish meaning | Spanish-friendly pronunciation note | Optional gesture |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ciao** | hola o adiós | `chao` | saludar con una mano |
| **buongiorno** | buenos días | `buon-yór-no` | dibujar un sol con las manos |
| **grazie** | gracias | `grát-sie` | una mano sobre el corazón |
| **per favore** | por favor | `per fa-vó-re` | abrir una mano con amabilidad |
| **arrivederci** | adiós / hasta luego | `a-rri-ve-dér-chi` | despedirse despacio con la mano |

Pronunciation remains subject to an Italian-language review before audio production.

## Accessibility and microcopy rules

- Button target: preferably 1–3 words; never translate fragments that are later concatenated.
- Safety instruction comes before the playful action.
- Alt text states information the image adds; decorative paper textures have empty alt text.
- Live-region messages state the result once, without celebratory punctuation chains.
- Destructive actions name scope and what is retained.
- Audio controls use explicit verbs: **Reproducir**, **Pausar**, **Repetir**. No autoplay.
- Captions preserve spoken words; meaningful sound cues also receive a short text label.
