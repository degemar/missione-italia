# R4 research — `VER-03` · El puente que volvió del río

**Mission:** `VER-03` · **Research date:** 1 October 2026 · **Runtime edited:** no

## Recommendation

Replace the current arch-counting role exercise with a complete restoration mystery narrated by Bussola. Five arches rise from an illustrated Adige, but their stones have lost their order. The bridge reveals that it is not the work of one moment: Roman limestone, medieval brick, a Renaissance reconstruction and a twentieth-century recovery all coexist in what visitors see today.

The emotional hook is verified history: after Ponte Pietra was destroyed in 1945, stones and bricks were recovered from the river, sorted, numbered and reused in its 1957–1959 reconstruction. The children restore the bridge inside the app, one authored scene at a time. Nobody assigns a role, chooses whether the family crosses, invents a comparison, validates an observation or supplies a story.

The core path is entirely illustrated and works anywhere. If the family opens the optional exterior window, show one quiet contextual line once, outside narration:

> Mirad desde donde ya estáis; la pista no necesita acercarse al agua.

Do not repeat railings, leaning, climbing, crossings, adults, phones, route choices or Thursday logistics in the child flow.

## What is weak in the current mission

- The title promises a crossing, but the content mainly administers whether to cross, view or use a card.
- The opening spends its best sentence on roles and safety instead of creating suspense.
- `Vigía`, `Detective` and `Guía` require somebody to assign work before anything happens.
- “Compara dos colores o materiales” leaves the most important historical explanation unwritten.
- The family is asked to select a viewpoint, mode and route; those are travel decisions, not a children's story.
- Counting five arches is appropriate for the four-year-old, but it is not enough for the seven- and nine-year-old.
- The existing fact says five arches and current access, but omits why the arches look different and how the bridge returned after 1945.
- The generic manual/card fallbacks feel like a lesser version. This mission can have one complete illustrated path in every setting.
- Two safety sentences, a car-trip replacement rule and repeated “safe” wording interrupt the reveal.
- “Cinco arcos, una pista de equipo” ends the task without resolving a mystery or changing Bussola's journey.

## Evidence boundaries and caveats

- Do not call all five present arches “original Roman arches.” Two visibly preserve Roman work; the other visible sections belong to later rebuildings, and the whole bridge underwent post-war reconstruction.
- Do not give a single exact foundation year. Official sources describe a first-century-BCE origin, while the Comune's detailed history notes scholarly uncertainty and a possible date before 89 BCE. “Nació en época romana” is accurate and sufficient.
- Keep `probablemente` or `alrededor de` with the two central arches and 1520.
- “Recovered from the river” applies to material after the 1945 destruction. It does not mean every current stone was recovered or that the river preserved the bridge intact.
- “Rebuilt with original materials” does not mean “untouched.” The richer idea is continuity through documented reuse.
- The small pier opening assists water flow during floods; do not promise that it prevents flooding.
- Do not describe the UNESCO listing as an award for Ponte Pietra alone. It is one element of the inscribed City of Verona.
- Current access, closures, river conditions and construction works are volatile. Keep them out of narration and scored choices.
- The speaking bridge, underwater archive, glowing numbers and Bussola's recovered arches are **NUESTRA HISTORIA**, not historical claims.

## Story direction

### Display title

**El puente que volvió del río**

Keep `VER-03`, chapter `verona`, scoring, save states, `reward.power = teamwork`, offline-critical status and the existing optional Thursday placement unchanged.

### Opening narration

> Al abrir la pista del Adigio, Bussola oye cinco golpes bajo el agua: **toc, toc, toc, toc, toc**. De la corriente aparecen cinco arcos desordenados y una voz de piedra susurra: **“No pertenezco a una sola época. Me rompieron, me recogieron pieza a pieza y volví.”** Para descubrir quién habla, tendremos que despertar los cinco arcos y devolver cada fragmento a su tiempo.

Primary CTA: **Despertar los arcos**

Visual grammar: an illustrated river runs across a folded paper stage. Five empty arch silhouettes rise above it. Every recovered piece carries both a texture and a symbol, so no decision depends only on colour or reading. Bussola remains the only character.

## Complete playable sequence

The core path lasts five to seven minutes. It uses app artwork, captions and optional narration; it never requires GPS, a real bridge view, crossing, live access, physical cards or adult improvisation.

### Scene 1 — Five knocks

Five arch silhouettes gently pulse one at a time. The child taps each; Bussola counts aloud and prints a large numeral inside it.

**Prompt:** “Despierta los cinco arcos.”

| Tap | Spoken response | Visual response |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | “Uno.” | One dot and numeral `1`. |
| 2 | “Dos.” | Two dots and numeral `2`. |
| 3 | “Tres.” | Three dots and numeral `3`. |
| 4 | “Cuatro.” | Four dots and numeral `4`. |
| 5 | “Cinco. El puente abre su memoria.” | All five silhouettes hold steady. |

This is the age-four path. Taps in any order advance the fixed count; there is no failure state and no instruction to find real arches.

### Scene 2 — The bridge with three voices

Two pale stone pieces, one tower-and-brick piece and two central brick pieces rise from the paper river.

**Narration:**

> Ponte Pietra no nació de una sola vez. Dos arcos conservan grandes bloques de piedra de época romana. El arco junto a la torre habla de **1298**. Los dos del centro recuerdan una reconstrucción de alrededor de **1520**. Cinco arcos, pero tres voces antiguas.

The app highlights the destination slots before each action. The child does not choose a historical interpretation; they complete three authored matches:

1. Drag or tap the **two limestone pieces** into the two-dot slot marked `ROMA`.
2. Place the **tower arch** in the one-dot slot marked `1298`.
3. Place the **two central brick pieces** in the two-dot slot marked `≈1520`.

For tap-only mode, selecting a piece makes its valid slot pulse. After one unsuccessful placement, Bussola says the exact distinguishing clue and the correct slot remains highlighted.

**Resolve:**

> Piedra romana, ladrillo medieval y arcos reconstruidos en el Renacimiento. El puente no es una fotografía de un solo día: es un álbum construido sobre el río.

### Scene 3 — The river archive

The assembled bridge fades into a monochrome 1945 paper silhouette. It separates into large blocks and sinks behind a blue paper layer; no explosion, weapons or people are animated.

**Narration:**

> En 1945, durante la guerra, Ponte Pietra quedó destruido. Muchas piedras y ladrillos cayeron al Adigio. Después fueron recuperados, ordenados y numerados para que pudieran encontrar de nuevo su lugar.

**Prompt:** “¿Cómo volvió el puente? Toca los pasos en orden.”

| Step ID | Illustrated card | Correct order | Resolve line |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `recover` | A block rising from paper water | 1 | “Primero: recuperar.” |
| `number` | The block receives a clear numeral | 2 | “Después: ordenar y numerar.” |
| `return` | The numbered block fits an arch plan | 3 | “Al final: devolver cada pieza al puente.” |

**Retry before `recover`:** “Las piezas siguen bajo el agua. Primero hay que encontrarlas.”

**Retry before `number`:** “La pieza ya volvió del río, pero aún no sabe dónde encaja.”

**Retry before `return`:** “El plano necesita una pieza identificada. Busca la que ya tiene número.”

After two retries, Bussola demonstrates the next step and normal progress continues. Nothing is timed and no error removes progress.

### Scene 4 — The little water window

The app zooms into the pier between the two pale arches. A small opening blinks like an eye, then becomes a simple cutaway with flowing water.

**Narration:**

> Entre los dos arcos de piedra hay una abertura pequeña. No es una ventana para mirar: cuando el Adigio crece, deja pasar parte del agua a través del puente.

**Prompt:** “¿Qué ayuda a pasar esa pequeña abertura?”

| Choice ID | Label / icon | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `high-water` | **Agua cuando el río crece** | correct |
| `bells` | **El sonido de las campanas** | retry |
| `birds` | **Los pájaros del tejado** | retry |

**Correct response:** “¡Agua en camino! Hasta un hueco pequeño puede ayudar a una obra enorme.”

**Retry:** “La pista está en la corriente azul que atraviesa la abertura.”

### Scene 5 — Roman and rebuilt

The restored bridge and its 1945 reconstruction plan appear side by side.

**Narration:**

> Entre 1957 y 1959, el puente fue reconstruido con documentación cuidadosa y muchas piezas originales recuperadas. Por eso puede conservar historia romana y, al mismo tiempo, ser un gran trabajo de reconstrucción.

**Prompt:** “¿Cómo pueden ser ciertas las dos cosas?”

| Choice ID | Label | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `old-pieces-careful-rebuild` | **Conserva piezas antiguas que fueron colocadas de nuevo con cuidado** | correct |
| `never-damaged` | **Nunca sufrió daños** | retry |
| `became-wood` | **Se cambió por completo por madera** | retry |

**Correct response:**

> Exacto. Reconstruir no borró la historia: permitió que muchas de sus piezas regresaran a ella.

**Retry for `never-damaged`:** “El archivo del río ya nos mostró las piezas caídas en 1945.”

**Retry for `became-wood`:** “Mira las texturas recuperadas: piedra clara y ladrillo.”

### Scene 6 — Optional exterior echo

Offer two equal, unscored buttons:

- **Ver los colores fuera**
- **Seguir con el puente ilustrado**

Only after the first is selected, show once:

> Mirad desde donde ya estáis; la pista no necesita acercarse al agua.

Then show: **“¿Aparece alguna de estas huellas?”**

- **Piedra clara** — “La voz romana sigue en el puente.”
- **Ladrillo rojo** — “Las reconstrucciones dejaron su propio color.”
- **Una pequeña abertura** — “La ventana del agua está despierta.”
- **Hoy no se distingue** — “El archivo ilustrado guarda las tres huellas.”

Every answer continues. The app neither checks nor stores location or asks anyone to confirm what another person saw.

### Scene 7 — Restore the five arches

The five numbered arch pieces return in a fixed scrambled row. Each valid placement completes one illuminated span. The task is deterministic: target silhouettes show texture, dots and symbols; an incorrect piece returns with a spoken clue.

When all five fit, four time seals appear beneath the bridge:

1. **ROMA** — pale block
2. **1298** — tower
3. **≈1520** — compass and ruler
4. **1957–1959** — numbered recovered stone

**Final prompt:** “¿Qué puente estaba hablando?”

- `ponte-pietra` — **Ponte Pietra** · correct
- `arena` — **La Arena** · retry
- `paper-bridge` — **Un puente inventado por Bussola** · retry

**Correct response:** “¡Ponte Pietra! Cinco arcos guardan una historia de más de dos mil años de cambios.”

**Retry for `arena`:** “La Arena tiene arcos, pero no cruza el Adigio.”

**Retry for `paper-bridge`:** “Bussola inventó el archivo mágico; el puente y su reconstrucción son reales.”

## Completion and reward

### Reveal narration

> La última piedra encaja. Los cinco arcos se encienden sobre el Adigio y Bussola escucha otra vez la voz del puente: **“No regresé porque el tiempo retrocediera. Regresé porque alguien guardó mi memoria, recuperó mis piezas y supo dónde devolverlas.”** La aguja marca Trabajo en equipo. **Cinco arcos, cuatro tiempos y un puente que volvió.**

**Success line:** “Cinco arcos. Cuatro tiempos. Un puente que volvió.”

**Stamp:** Keep the stable `kind-navigator` ID, but redesign its face as a numbered stone joining two half-arches. Child-facing stamp name: **Restauradores del tiempo**.

**Discovery fact:** “Ponte Pietra reúne partes de varias épocas. Tras su destrucción en 1945, muchos materiales originales se recuperaron del Adigio y se reutilizaron en la reconstrucción de 1957–1959.”

**Optional curiosity card:**

> **PONS LAPIDEUS** · En la Edad Media se conocía como el “puente de piedra”. Su nombre actual, Ponte Pietra, conserva esa misma idea.

## Fallbacks that preserve the story

- **Not at Ponte Pietra / no GPS:** play the identical illustrated sequence; location is never checked.
- **Crowd / closure / temporary works:** skip the exterior echo automatically or by one visible button; the seven core scenes remain unchanged.
- **Rain / tired legs / hotel:** identical illustrated mission. Offer larger targets and a single-tap placement mode, not a reduced “manual” story.
- **No audio:** exact captions, count labels, textures and symbols preserve every clue.
- **Offline:** cache the five arches, three era seals, river-archive sequence, water-window diagram, reconstruction plan, narration and captions.
- **Reduced motion:** replace sinking/rising pieces with adjacent before/after panels and short cross-fades.
- **Four-year-old route:** the five-count opening and silhouette matching can be completed by shape and dots; Bussola demonstrates historical ordering after retries.

Remove “el adulto elige,” role assignment, free comparison, route selection, crossing decisions, physical cards, manual validation and safety recitals from the child experience.

- The app clearly distinguishes the bridge's Roman fabric, later visible phases and post-war reconstruction without calling the whole bridge untouched Roman work.

- The same full path works at the bridge, in the hotel, offline, muted and without GPS.
- The optional exterior echo cannot block completion and has one non-narrated contextual line only.

- Narration and captions are exact pairs; no fact or answer exists only in sound, colour or motion.
- Runtime remains untouched until the R4 implementation wave consumes this brief.
