# R4 research — VER-01 · El ala que no vuela

**Mission:** `VER-01` · **Research date:** 1 October 2026 · **Runtime edited:** no

## Recommendation

Replace the current role-assignment exercise with a narrated mystery built around the Arena's most child-friendly real detail: only a short section of the original outer façade survives, and it is called the **Ala** — the Wing. Bussola mistakes it for a giant bird's wing; the children discover that it is made of stone, decode its white-and-pink palette, restore three moments to a time line and unlock one surprising crowd fact.

The app supplies the story, observation target, illustrated fallback, questions, answers, hints and resolution. Nobody assigns jobs, chooses a viewpoint, invents a tale, explains a pattern or decides what the next activity should be.

Use one contextual cue, once, immediately before looking at the monument:

> Desde la plaza y sin tocar la piedra, buscad el muro que sobresale.

Do not repeat generic supervision, climbing, phone-holding, ticket or closure paragraphs inside the story, quiz, fallback or celebration.

## What is weak in the current mission

- The title promises a time machine, but the current experience is only “find an arch and compare two things.”
- The story gives instructions rather than creating suspense, a character problem or a reveal.
- `Vigía`, `Detective` and `Guía` make the family administer the game. “Elige con el adulto” leaves a core decision unwritten.
- “Elemento moderno de eventos” is vague and may not be visible from every exterior position.
- The mission ends after one observation and one familiar date. It misses the **Ala**, the two local stone colours, the 1913 opera turning point and the 30,000-person scale.
- Four generic fallbacks describe procedures rather than preserving the adventure.
- Two safety statements interrupt a low-risk observation that can use one short, contextual line.
- “Amable máquina del tiempo” tells the tone instead of making the time travel happen.

## History and imagination boundary

The story should make its fiction delightful and unmistakable:

- **HECHO:** the first-century date, elliptical form, approximate ancient capacity, surviving **Ala**, white-and-pink stone, 1913 *Aida* and continued use for performances.
- **NUESTRA HISTORIA:** Bussola sneezes imaginary Roman dust, its needle draws a feather, the stone sends a message, three echoes escape and a door through time opens. These are Missione Italia fiction.
- **Do not collapse disputed dating:** the Fondazione page gives a more specific 30–42 AD range from a coin, while civic archaeology pages use “first century” or “around the middle of the first century.” The child fact should retain the cautious civic wording.
- **Do not invent a single destruction story:** the loss of the outer ring spans demolition, stone reuse and earthquake damage. The mission only needs the observable truth that one short section remains.
- **Do not romanticise violence:** ancient programming can be described as “espectáculos públicos” in the core mission. Gladiators and animal hunts are unnecessary here.
- **Do not claim perfect acoustics:** Fondazione Arena uses promotional language about sound. It is not needed for this mission and should not become a graded fact.
- **Do not equate current seats with ancient capacity:** the approximately 30,000 figure is the historical capacity described by the museum.

## Story direction

### Display title

**El ala que no vuela**

Keep `VER-01`, `verona`, `teamwork`, `pattern-maker`, completion states and scoring identity unchanged.

### Opening narration

> Al llegar a Piazza Bra, Bussola estornuda una nube de polvo imaginario. Su aguja ya no señala el norte: dibuja una pluma. Entonces aparece un mensaje: **“De mi gran fachada solo queda el Ala.”** Bussola busca un pájaro gigantesco, pero la Arena guarda otro secreto. Para reactivar su máquina del tiempo tendréis que encontrar un ala sin plumas, descifrar dos colores de piedra y ordenar tres momentos separados por casi dos mil años. Si el código encaja, la Arena devolverá su último eco.

Primary CTA: **Seguir la pluma**

Visual grammar: the compass needle bends into a paper feather; the feather lands over an incomplete line drawing of the Arena. Keep the **Ala** silhouette hidden until the observation reveal.

## Complete playable sequence

The core path lasts five to seven minutes and completes outside or entirely from cached illustrations. It never needs GPS, entry, tickets, live data or parent-created content.

### Beat 1 — The impossible message

The message arrives in three stamped fragments:

1. **ALA** — “Pertenece a la Arena.”
2. **PIEDRA** — “Tiene dos tonos.”
3. **TIEMPO** — “Guarda tres ecos.”

Narration:

> Ala, piedra, tiempo. Bussola conoce alas de pájaro y alas de avión, pero ninguna hecha de roca. La primera pieza del código está ahí fuera.

CTA: **Buscar el Ala**

### Beat 2 — Outdoor observation

Show the contextual line once:

> Desde la plaza y sin tocar la piedra, buscad el muro que sobresale.

Then dim the screen and show only:

- **La hemos visto**
- **Ver la pista dibujada**

The second action opens an original outline of the Arena with the surviving **Ala** highlighted. Both buttons continue to the same puzzle; neither is labelled as failure or manual mode.

Do not ask the family to select a viewpoint, assign a navigator, verify proximity or identify modern staging.

### Beat 3 — Which wing survived?

The app presents three original silhouettes.

**Prompt:** “¿Cuál es el Ala de la Arena?”

| Choice ID | Illustrated answer | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `outer-wall-fragment` | **El muro alto que aún sobresale** | Correct |
| `stage-scenery` | **Un decorado junto al escenario** | Retry |
| `stone-bird` | **Un pájaro tallado en piedra** | Retry |

**Correct response:**

> ¡Un ala sin plumas! El **Ala** es el breve tramo que queda de la antigua fachada exterior de la Arena.

**Retry for `stage-scenery`:**

> Ese elemento puede cambiar con cada espectáculo. El Ala lleva siglos unida al monumento.

**Retry for `stone-bird`:**

> Bussola también buscó un pájaro. Esta Ala es un fragmento de muro.

### Beat 4 — The stone palette

The **Ala** breaks into softly textured colour tiles. Every option also carries a distinct pattern, so colour is never the only signal.

**Prompt:** “¿Qué pareja pertenece a la piedra exterior?”

1. **Blanco + rosado** — smooth stripes; correct
2. **Azul + verde** — waves; retry
3. **Negro + dorado** — stars; retry

**Correct response:**

> Blanco y rosado. La fachada usó piedra de las canteras cercanas de Valpolicella. El tiempo ha suavizado los colores, pero el código sigue ahí.

**Retry:**

> Busca los tonos claros de la piedra. No tienen que parecer colores recién pintados.

The illustrated version must make the answer solvable even when rain, distance, restoration, staging or lighting obscure the real palette.

### Beat 5 — Restore the three echoes

Three large cards tumble out of order. Each carries a numeral, an image and a read-aloud label.

**Prompt:** “Ordenad los ecos: del más antiguo al más reciente.”

| Card ID | Card copy | Illustration |
|---|---|---|
| `roman-century` | **SIGLO I · Los romanos construyen el anfiteatro.** | white-and-pink stones forming an oval |
| `aida-1913` | **1913 · Aida inaugura la tradición de ópera.** | a paper stage, one music note and `1913` |
| `arena-today` | **HOY · La Arena sigue reuniendo espectáculos.** | the same oval glowing beneath a night sky |

Correct order: `roman-century → aida-1913 → arena-today`.

**Success response:**

> Piedra, música, presente: casi dos mil años han vuelto a su sitio.

**Retry behavior:** the cards spring gently back and Bussola says:

> Empieza por el número romano más antiguo: **SIGLO I**. Después busca **1913** y termina en **HOY**.

Do not ask an adult to explain centuries. The cards themselves contain the ordering information.

### Beat 6 — Bussola underestimates the crowd

Bussola draws 300 tiny spectators and proudly announces that the Arena is full. The drawing occupies only a small piece of the oval.

**Prompt:** “En época romana, ¿aproximadamente cuántas personas podía reunir?”

1. **300** — one small crowd block
2. **3.000** — ten crowd blocks
3. **30.000** — the oval filled; correct

**Correct response:**

> ¡Unas 30.000! Era uno de los grandes anfiteatros de Italia. A Bussola le faltaban dos ceros y muchísimos bocadillos.

**Retry:**

> Era una multitud enorme. Elige el dibujo que llena casi todo el óvalo.

The visual density makes this playable for the four-year-old without requiring place-value knowledge.

## Completion and reward

### Reveal narration

> La pluma de Bussola se vuelve piedra blanca y rosada. Los tres ecos entran en orden: siglo I, 1913, hoy. La aguja da una vuelta completa y dibuja el óvalo de la Arena con una línea de luz. **“¡Entendido!”**, dice Bussola. **“No todas las alas vuelan. Algunas sostienen casi dos mil años de historias.”** El Ala encaja en el pasaporte y se convierte en el sello Constructor de Patrones.

**Success line:** “Ala encontrada. Los tres ecos vuelven a estar en su tiempo.”

**Stamp:** keep `pattern-maker`; redesign it as a white-and-pink stone arch with a small feather-shaped gap.

**Discovery fact:** “La Arena se construyó en el siglo I. Del anillo exterior queda un breve tramo llamado Ala.”

**Optional curiosity card:**

> **PARA MENTES CURIOSAS** · La Arena se levantó fuera de las murallas romanas para que el público pudiera llegar sin llenar el centro de la ciudad.

## Fallbacks that preserve the story

- **Arena not visible / crowded / rain / tired legs:** `Ver la pista dibujada` opens the highlighted Arena outline and continues through every beat. Do not show a “manual completion” lecture.
- **Modern staging hides details:** the app never requires a pristine exterior; the silhouette and palette cards remain authoritative for play.
- **No GPS:** irrelevant; no location check exists.
- **Closed venue:** irrelevant; the complete mission is exterior/illustration-based.
- **Offline:** cache the Arena outline, three **Ala** silhouettes, patterned palette tiles, time cards, crowd cards, narration and captions.
- **Reduced motion:** reveal the feather, silhouettes, cards and stamp with short cross-fades. Do not spin the compass or scatter the time cards.
- **Audio unavailable:** captions and visual stamps carry every clue; no sound recognition is required.

These are authored branches. None asks a parent to make up content, select a role, explain history or judge an answer.
