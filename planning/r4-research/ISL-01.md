# R4 research — ISL-01 · El secreto de las mil flores

**Mission:** `ISL-01` · **Research date:** 1 October 2026 · **Runtime edited:** no

## Recommendation

Replace the generic pattern observation exercise with a self-contained maker mystery about **murrine** and **millefiori**. Bussola discovers an imaginary glass cane whose design has been scrambled. The app reveals a visual recipe, lets the children rebuild its concentric layers, stretches the cane, and asks them to predict the pattern hidden in the next slice. The answer unlocks the meaning of *millefiori*: “mil flores.”

The app tells the whole story, presents every visual choice, checks the puzzle, supplies hints, and performs the reveal. Nobody assigns roles, selects an object, invents a story, judges an observation, or creates a fallback. A museum, shop, furnace demonstration, purchase, photo, GPS position, or live service is never required.

The only contextual line needed is part of the optional outdoor bonus: **“El vidrio se mira con los ojos; el dibujo se queda donde está.”** Do not repeat it during the core story, quiz, explanation, or celebration.

## What is weak in the current mission

- “Laboratorio de patrones de vidrio” sounds like a classroom activity rather than an adventure.
- The story explains procedure and assigns `Vigía`, `Detective` and `Guía` instead of creating a mystery.
- “Cualquier lugar elegido por el adulto” leaves the main creative decision outside the product.
- Finding an unspecified reflection is difficult to verify and teaches almost nothing about Murano glass.
- The current three-card pattern is arbitrary; circle-star-circle has no meaningful connection to a documented glass technique.
- The two facts are correct but disconnected from the activity. The museum date does not explain why patterns matter.
- Four fallback paragraphs and two safety instructions interrupt a ten-minute experience.
- “El nuevo patrón pertenece al equipo” promises creativity but the app does not actually help the children design anything.
- The mission misses a remarkable age-friendly idea: a complex picture can run through a long glass cane so that every cross-cut slice reveals the same tiny design.

## Craft and history guardrails

- **Murrine were not invented in nineteenth-century Murano.** The mosaic-glass method is ancient; Venetian makers used it in the fifteenth century and Murano makers revived and modernised it in the nineteenth.
- **Millefiori is a technique/effect, not the name of every colourful Murano object.** The story may call its fictional cane “la caña de las mil flores,” but the fact card must use the accurate term.
- **Not all Murano glass is blown.** Murrine, beads, filigree and blown forms involve different processes. Do not use “soplar vidrio” as a universal explanation.
- **A repeated cross-section is the key property of a well-made patterned cane.** The app can simplify the geometry, but the identical-slice puzzle should preserve that idea.
- **Heat joins and reshapes the real material.** Children only create a digital design; never say they have “made Murano glass.” Say they have decoded or designed a Murano-inspired pattern.
- **Murano origin is not authenticated by appearance.** Do not ask the family to identify “real Murano glass” in a shop window or imply that the app can verify merchandise.
- **The furnace transfer has a broad official date.** Say “a finales del siglo XIII,” not “en 1291,” unless the exact decree is independently sourced.
- **Marietta Barovier’s attribution is suitable as a museum-backed fact**, but the mission should not imply that she invented all glass beads or all millefiori.
- No workshop is assumed to admit visitors or run a demonstration. No displayed object should be touched, moved, photographed, or used as a completion gate.

## Story direction

### Display title

**El secreto de las mil flores**

Keep `ISL-01`, `lagoon-islands`, `creativity`, `pattern-maker`, completion states, scoring and offline-critical status unchanged.

### Opening narration

> Al llegar a Murano, la esfera de Bussola se vuelve transparente. Dentro aparece una varita de vidrio imaginaria y una voz diminuta susurra: **“Guardo mil flores, pero no tengo ni una hoja.”** La varita gira y enseña una estrella dentro de otra… hasta que sus colores saltan y se mezclan. Solo quien reconstruya su dibujo podrá descubrir dónde se esconden las flores y encender la primera chispa de Creatividad.

Primary CTA: **Abrir la varita secreta**

Visual grammar: a long translucent cane lies diagonally across the screen. Its near end shows a blurred circular cross-section. Three coloured “glass” layers have escaped and hover like paper cut-outs. No furnace, realistic hot glass, branded product, or shop scene is needed.

## Complete playable sequence

The core path lasts five to six minutes and is completely playable from the cached app. The youngest child can solve the large-shape steps; older children get the prediction and fact reveal.

### Beat 1 — The riddle inside the cane

The cane rotates without revealing its end.

Narration:

> Por fuera parece una varita de rayas. Pero su verdadero secreto no está en los lados. Bussola encuentra tres pistas: **CENTRO**, **ANILLO** y **PÉTALOS**. Juntas forman un dibujo escondido.

Show the visual recipe as three large fixed clues:

1. **CENTRO** — yellow five-point star
2. **ANILLO** — red ring
3. **PÉTALOS** — six blue rounded points

The recipe remains visible throughout the build. No one must remember spoken information to continue.

CTA: **Reconstruir el dibujo**

### Beat 2 — Build the murrina

The app presents one empty circular cross-section. Each round asks for exactly one layer with three large illustrated choices.

**Round A — “¿Qué va en el centro?”**

- yellow star — correct
- blue circle — retry
- red wave — retry

**Round B — “¿Qué rodea la estrella?”**

- red ring — correct
- blue triangle — retry
- yellow dots — retry

**Round C — “¿Qué aparece por fuera?”**

- six blue petals — correct
- red square frame — retry
- yellow zigzag — retry

The app-led retry always points back to the pictured recipe:

> La varita no regaña; vuelve a enseñar su pista. Busca la forma que ocupa este lugar.

Each correct answer clicks into place with a glassy chime and a strong shape outline. Colour never carries the answer alone.

After Round C:

> ¡El dibujo ha vuelto! Una estrella, un anillo y seis pétalos forman una pequeña roseta. Pero Bussola todavía no encuentra las mil flores.

### Beat 3 — Stretch the secret

The completed disc folds into the end of a short cane. The child drags a large handle to stretch it; a tap alternative labelled **Estirar** performs the same action.

Narration:

> En una caña de vidrio real, las capas calientes se unen y la caña se estira con enorme habilidad. El dibujo se hace pequeño, pero sigue viajando por dentro.

This is an explanatory animation, not a simulation of a full manufacturing process. Reduced-motion mode swaps the drag animation for three static stages: short cane, long cane, cut slices.

### Beat 4 — Predict the next slice

The app cuts the fictional cane into three illustrated discs. The first two reveal the same star-ring-petal pattern; the third remains face down.

**Prompt:** “Si el dibujo viaja por toda la caña, ¿qué aparecerá en la tercera rodaja?”

| Stable new choice ID | Card | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `same-rosetta` | the same yellow star, red ring and six blue petals | correct |
| `swapped-layers` | blue centre, yellow ring, red petals | retry |
| `empty-glass` | transparent disc without a motif | retry |

**Correct response:**

> ¡La misma roseta! Al cortar una caña de patrón, cada sección revela el dibujo que recorría su interior.

**Retry for `swapped-layers`:**

> Las capas no cambian de sitio mientras la caña se alarga. Sigue el camino de la estrella amarilla.

**Retry for `empty-glass`:**

> El secreto no estaba pintado solo en la punta: viajaba por dentro de la caña.

### Beat 5 — The thousand-flower reveal

The three discs multiply into an illustrated mosaic while retaining clear outlines and limited motion.

Narration:

> En Murano, las secciones de cañas con capas de colores pueden convertirse en **murrine**. Cuando muchas estrellas y flores cubren una pieza, el efecto se llama **millefiori**: “mil flores”. No crecieron en tierra. Crecieron dentro de un dibujo de vidrio.

CTA: **Crear nuestra flor de Bussola**

### Beat 6 — Authored creative choice

Creativity belongs to the children, but the app supplies the complete canvas and bounded choices. Each child taps one layer in sequence; no names, roles, or parent assignment appear.

1. choose one centre: **sol**, **estrella**, **gota**
2. choose one middle line: **anillo**, **onda**, **triángulos**
3. choose one outside shape: **pétalos**, **rayos**, **burbujas**

All 27 combinations are valid. The result becomes the family’s digital `pattern-maker` stamp. The app automatically advances after each of the three saved choices, so nobody must decide who goes next or narrate what the design means.

Final narration adapts only the three selected labels:

> Centro de {centre}, borde de {middle}, corona de {outside}. Esta flor no copia ninguna vitrina: es el diseño que vuestra expedición ha dejado en Bussola.

## Optional outdoor bonus

After the core completion, show one optional card that never changes the score:

**“¿Murano os enseña otro patrón?”**

> Buscad desde el camino una estrella, una flor, una espiral o varios círculos repetidos. Puede estar en vidrio, piedra, una reja o un reflejo. El vidrio se mira con los ojos; el dibujo se queda donde está.

Four self-report buttons — **estrella**, **flor**, **espiral**, **círculos** — all produce a tiny matching sparkle on the stamp. A fifth button, **seguir la historia**, closes the card with no penalty. The app does not ask an adult to choose a display or verify the answer.

## Completion and reward

### Reveal narration

> La flor digital entra en la esfera de Bussola. Primero parece una sola; después se refleja en diez, en cien, en mil. La esfera ya no está vacía: guarda una idea capaz de repetirse sin perder su forma. Murano ha enseñado a Bussola que crear también significa combinar, probar y transformar. La primera chispa de Creatividad está encendida.

**Success line:** “La flor escondida se ha multiplicado: Creatividad encendida.”

**Stamp:** Keep `pattern-maker`; redesign it from a generic pattern into the exact three-layer motif selected in Beat 6.

**Discovery fact:** “Las cañas rosetta de Murano se forman con capas de vidrio de distintos colores; al cortarlas aparecen estrellas concéntricas que pueden crear el efecto millefiori.”

**Optional maker card:**

> **UNA CREADORA DE MURANO** · El Museo del Vidrio atribuye a Marietta Barovier la invención, en el siglo XV, de las perlas rosetta hechas con capas de vidrio de varios colores. En Murano, las ideas también viajaron de una generación de artistas a la siguiente.

**Optional museum card:**

> **APRENDER MIRANDO** · El Museo del Vidrio nació en 1861. Un año después abrió junto a él una escuela donde los vidrieros estudiaban dibujo y piezas antiguas en sus días libres.

## Fallbacks that preserve the story

- **No shop, glass display, museum, or workshop encountered:** irrelevant; Beats 1–6 are the complete mission.
- **Rain or tired legs:** play the same cached core while seated. Do not substitute a generic “manual completion.”
- **No GPS:** irrelevant; no location check exists.
- **Closed museum or furnace:** irrelevant; neither is a dependency.
- **Offline:** cache the layer cards, cane stages, slice quiz, 27 stamp combinations, narration and captions.
- **Reduced motion:** use three static cane stages and a cross-fade from one disc to a small mosaic; no stretching or multiplying animation.
- **Sound off:** captions and visual recipe contain every clue.

Fallbacks are authored presentation states, not instructions for a parent to invent an equivalent activity.
