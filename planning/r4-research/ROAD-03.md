# R4 research — ROAD-03 · El teatrino de las cinco palabras

**Mission:** `ROAD-03` · **Research date:** 1 October 2026 · **Runtime edited:** no

## Recommendation

Replace the current role-assignment exercise with a complete five-scene micro-story. Bussola has lost her voice behind a paper theatre curtain; every correctly matched Italian word switches on one stage light. The app narrates the story, plays the words, asks every question, supplies every gesture and resolves every answer. Parents do not invent dialogue, assign roles or decide what happens next.

No mission-level safety copy is needed. This is a seated, location-independent listening and matching game. Any global driving lock belongs in the app shell, outside the story.

## What is weak in the current mission

- The current beat explains a teaching exercise instead of beginning an adventure.
- `spotter`, `detective` and `navigator` make a parent distribute jobs before play can begin.
- “Elegid una frase que la familia pueda usar después” leaves the creative payoff unwritten.
- “Nadie tiene que hablar con desconocidos” and “el adulto lee…” interrupt a harmless language game.
- The current pronunciation `buón-yor-no` puts the emphasis on the wrong syllable. Standard pronunciation stresses **gior**: `/bwɔnˈdʒorno/`.
- The current copy translates `arrivederci` as only “adiós”; “hasta volvernos a ver” better preserves its friendly expectation of meeting again.
- The mission misses its best travel connection: `ciao` is Venetian in origin, an elegant bridge toward the family’s Venice chapter.

## Language findings

| Phrase | Child-facing meaning | Pronunciation aid for Spanish readers | Usage that the story should teach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ciao** | hola / adiós | **CHAO** · one beat | Friendly and informal; used both when meeting and parting, normally with people addressed as `tu`. Treccani traces it to Venetian `s-ciao`. |
| **buongiorno** | buenos días; saludo cortés de día | buon-**YÓR**-no | A neutral greeting, especially in the first part of the day. The switch to `buonasera` varies by person and region, so the game must not impose a precise cutoff hour. |
| **grazie** | gracias | **GRÁ**-tsie · two beats | Expresses gratitude. The `z` is a compact `ts` sound; do not stretch it into a separate Spanish `si-e`. |
| **per favore** | por favor | per fa-**VÓ**-re | Courtesy formula used when asking for something. The stress belongs on `vo` in `favore`. |
| **arrivederci** | hasta volvernos a ver / adiós | ar-ri-ve-**DÉR**-chi | A parting greeting that carries the hope or expectation of meeting again. The ending `ci` sounds like Spanish `chi`. |

The pronunciation aids are deliberately readable approximations, not phonetic notation and not a basis for grading a child’s accent. Italian audio should be the model; repetition should never use speech recognition or correctness scoring.

### Register caveats

- Do not present `ciao` as the universal greeting for every person. It is confiding and informal; `buongiorno` is the safer neutral greeting in the story’s shop scene.
- Do not claim that `buongiorno` stops at noon. Treccani describes regional and individual variation in the transition to `buonasera`.
- Do not introduce `arrivederla` in this first mission. It is a more courteous singular form for a person addressed with `Lei`, but it adds a grammar branch that distracts from the five-word story.
- Keep `prego` for a later bonus, if ever. Adding it here would weaken the five-light rhythm.
- The Venetian etymology can be simplified to “`ciao` nació en el habla veneciana y viajó por el mundo.” Avoid explaining the historical literal derivation to young children; it adds no useful play value.

## Story direction

### Title

**El teatrino de las cinco palabras**

### Opening narration

> En algún punto del camino, Bussola intenta saludar a Italia… pero de su boca solo sale una nubecita de papel. Su voz se ha escondido detrás del telón. Cinco focos esperan cinco palabras italianas. Cada palabra correcta encenderá una luz. ¿Abrimos el teatrino?

Primary CTA: **Abrir el telón**

Visual grammar: a miniature paper theatre inside Bussola, five empty marquee bulbs, one illustrated prop per word, and a final stamp that assembles from the five lit symbols.

## Complete playable sequence

The sequence is linear and lasts about three minutes. The app reads each line and advances after the child chooses an answer. No setup screen, role picker or family instruction is required.

### Scene 1 — The sun lamp

**Narration**

> El primer foco tiene forma de sol. El día empieza, el telón tiembla y Bussola necesita un saludo luminoso.

**Prompt:** “¿Qué palabra abre el día?”

**Choices:** `buongiorno` · `grazie` · `arrivederci`

**Correct beat:** Play **buongiorno** once, illuminate the sun and animate two hands drawing a small sunrise.

**Response:** “¡Buon-YÓR-no! El sol de papel ya brilla.”

**Retry:** “Busca el saludo que lleva un día bueno dentro: buon… giorno.”

### Scene 2 — The returned bell

**Narration**

> Una estrella de cartón encuentra la campanita perdida de Bussola y se la devuelve. El segundo foco espera una palabra agradecida.

**Prompt:** “¿Qué dice Bussola?”

**Choices:** `grazie` · `ciao` · `per favore`

**Correct beat:** Play **grazie**, ring a soft two-note chime and light the heart-shaped bulb.

**Response:** “¡GRÁ-tsie! Una palabra pequeña puede llevar un agradecimiento enorme.”

**Retry:** “La campanita ha vuelto. Busca la palabra que significa «gracias».”

### Scene 3 — The silver key

**Narration**

> La puerta del camerino está cerrada. La llave cuelga de una luna de papel. Bussola sabe pedirla con amabilidad.

**Prompt:** “La llave, ___.”

**Choices:** `per favore` · `buongiorno` · `arrivederci`

**Correct beat:** Play **per favore**, slide the key into Bussola’s open palm and light the hand-shaped bulb.

**Response:** “Per fa-VÓ-re. La puerta se abre sin empujar.”

**Retry:** “Busca las dos palabras que convierten una petición en algo amable.”

### Scene 4 — The two-sided mask

**Narration**

> Entra una máscara amiga y otra se prepara para salir. Las dos llevan la misma palabra pintada: sirve al llegar y al despedirse entre amigos.

**Prompt:** “¿Qué palabra tiene dos puertas?”

**Choices:** `ciao` · `grazie` · `buongiorno`

**Correct beat:** Play **ciao**, flip the mask from an entering face to a waving face and light the double-door bulb.

**Response:** “¡CHAO! Hola por una puerta, adiós por la otra.”

**Retry:** “Es una palabra breve que puede decir «hola» y también «adiós».”

### Scene 5 — The ribbon that returns

**Narration**

> El teatrino está a punto de cerrar, pero esta historia no termina para siempre. Una cinta sale de Bussola, da una vuelta y regresa.

**Prompt:** “¿Qué despedida promete volver a verse?”

**Choices:** `arrivederci` · `per favore` · `grazie`

**Correct beat:** Play **arrivederci**, loop the ribbon back toward the stage and light the final bulb.

**Response:** “Ar-ri-ve-DÉR-chi: hasta volvernos a ver.”

**Retry:** “Busca la palabra larga que guarda un próximo encuentro.”

## Finale quiz — The first real line

This single context question closes the learning loop without sending the family to perform it in public.

**Narration**

> Los cinco focos brillan. Antes de devolverle la voz, el teatrino muestra su escena final: Bussola entra por la mañana en una pequeña tienda italiana y ve a alguien que todavía no conoce.

**Prompt:** “¿Cómo empieza Bussola?”

**Choices:**

1. `Buongiorno` — correct
2. `Ciao` — friendly, but not the neutral first choice taught for this scene
3. `Arrivederci` — closes an encounter rather than opening it

**Correct response:** “Buongiorno. La voz de Bussola ha vuelto.”

**Incorrect response for `ciao`:** “Ciao funciona entre amigos. Aquí Bussola acaba de conocer a alguien: prueba el saludo más neutro.”

**Incorrect response for `arrivederci`:** “Arrivederci espera al final. Bussola acaba de entrar.”

## Reward and cultural reveal

**Reveal narration**

> ¡Los cinco focos están encendidos! Y Bussola descubre un secreto: `ciao` nació en el habla veneciana y viajó tan lejos que hoy se reconoce en muchos lugares del mundo. La palabra ya conocía el camino hacia Venecia.

**Success line:** “Cinco palabras, cinco luces: la Curiosidad ya tiene voz.”

**Stamp:** Keep `team-spark`, but redraw it as a lit paper marquee with the five symbols: sun, heart, hand, double door and returning ribbon.

## Interaction and audio specification

- One large illustrated choice per word; always show the Italian spelling beside its symbol.
- Play a short native-quality Italian clip after selection, never before the first guess in scenes 1–5. Provide a replay button labelled **Oír otra vez**.
- Narration remains Spanish; the five target clips use an Italian voice. Cache all six assets for offline play.
- On first playback, highlight syllables in time with the clip. Capitalisation in pronunciation aids marks stress, not shouting.
- A tap on the active prop performs the gesture automatically; nobody has to allocate a role.
- The four-year-old can solve through symbols and animation; older children get the context distinctions in the prompts and finale.
- Repetition may be invited with **Ahora dilo con Bussola**, followed immediately by **Continuar**. Do not listen, record or score speech.
- Wrong answers should advance the theatre animation slightly, then offer the story-specific hint above. No generic “incorrect” buzzer.
- Reduced-motion mode replaces flips, loops and slides with cross-fades while preserving the five-light progress state.
